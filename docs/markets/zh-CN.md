# China — CV guide (zh-CN)

**Market snapshot:** Chinese campus recruiting expects a compact Chinese CV,
usually **one A4 page** (two only with substantial experience). Education comes
first for fresh graduates, followed by internships, projects, awards and
certificates. A professional ID photo is usually expected in campus and
state-owned applications, but it is not a universal legal requirement. Prefer
a **single-column** layout: sidebars and creative two-column designs are not
the norm outside design roles. Keep personal data minimal: name, phone and
email are enough for most private employers.

Last reviewed: 2026-11-19.

## Sections and order

1. **Personal information** — name, phone, email and city; a professional ID
   photo (usually expected in campus and state-owned applications).
2. **Job objective (求职意向)** — the target role, city and availability.
3. **Education (教育经历)** — school, major, degree and dates; first for
   students and recent graduates.
4. **Internships and work experience (实习/工作经历)** — reverse
   chronological; use the STAR method and numbers.
5. **Projects and campus experience (项目/校园经历)** — research, competitions,
   student organizations and volunteering.
6. **Awards and certificates (荣誉/证书)** — scholarships, contests,
   certificates, publications.
7. **Skills and languages (技能/语言)** — tools, programming languages,
   English level and other languages.
8. **Self-evaluation (自我评价)** — optional; three to five concrete lines.

For experienced hires, move work experience before education. Tailor the
“job objective” and keywords to each posting.

## Photo and personal data

A photo is **not a legal requirement** and practice varies by industry.
Chinese career advice notes that it is not a default rule, and some technology
candidates are advised to leave it out; however, campus recruiting, state-owned
enterprises, traditional industries and client-facing roles commonly expect a
professional ID photo, and many templates reserve a slot for one. Practical
rule: include a formal, recent ID photo unless the posting says otherwise.
Never use a selfie, a holiday photo or a heavily filtered image.

Keep the personal block short: name, phone and email. Do not write an ID
number, full home address, health status or marital status. “Political status”
(政治面貌) is relevant mainly for state-owned enterprises and civil-service
roles; for most private employers it is optional and often better left out.
Hometown (籍贯) is usually unnecessary and can invite bias.

## Format and platforms

- Simple black text, consistent fonts (宋体 for body, 黑体 for headings are
  common), 10–12 pt body.
- **Prefer a single-column layout.** Sidebars, two-column/creative layouts,
  tables and text boxes are not the norm for campus recruiting and make ATS
  parsing less reliable; design/creative roles are the exception.
- One page is the norm for campus recruiting; avoid a “one and a half page”.
- Use standard section headings and tailor keywords to the job description.
- Chinese employers often recruit through 校园招聘, BOSS直聘, 智联招聘 and
  51job; some also ask for a PDF emailed directly.
- If an English CV is requested, send one page in Chinese and one in English;
  Chinese first unless the employer says otherwise.
- Name the file with your name, school, major, degree and target role.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a long “autobiography” instead of a one-page CV.
- Listing every personal detail, including ID number and family information.
- Using a sidebar or a decorative two-column layout for a campus/state-owned
  application.
- Using decorative fonts, colors or graphics that distract from the content.
- Copying a generic self-evaluation.
- Forgetting to tailor the job objective and keywords.
- Typos, inconsistent punctuation or font sizes.

## CVLaTeX settings

- Template: **Chinese (standard)**, **Black tabs**, **Blue ribbon**, **Navy
  minimal**, **Playful** or **Sword**. For campus/state-owned applications,
  prefer a single-column Chinese-style template; the sidebar templates are less
  common in this market.
- Paper: **A4**.
- Font size: **10–12 pt**; font style: template default.
- CV language: **Chinese**.
- Photo: recommended for campus/state-owned roles; optional for some
  technology/private roles.
- Export: **PDF**.
